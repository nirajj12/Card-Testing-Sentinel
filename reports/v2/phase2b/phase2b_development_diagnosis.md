# Phase 2B Development-Only Diagnosis

**Status: development-only diagnostic analysis of already-known evidence.**
Every number in this report is computed from the training population and the
already-inspected, already-blocked Phase 2 validation population (device
splits and features that were generated once, under the frozen Phase 1
generator, and whose validation result is already known and closed per
`reports/v2/modeling/phase2_final_closeout.md`). **None of the numbers below
are, or should ever be cited as, fresh validation performance.** No new data
was generated. No model was retrained. No freeze or blind challenge was
created or accessed. Raw card tokens, IPs, CVV, expiry, and other sensitive
values are not present in any output table; all tables are aggregated by
`scenario_tag`/`population`/`fold`, never by individual device or session id.

Evidence artifacts backing this report (all new, under
`artifacts/v2/phase2b/diagnostics/`, produced by
`scripts/v2/phase2b/diagnose_phase2_development_evidence.py`):
`device_authorization_counts.csv`, `score_distributions.csv`,
`fp_fn_by_scenario_at_review_threshold_0_45.csv`,
`feature_distribution_comparison.csv`,
`grouped_oof_metrics_by_fold_and_scenario.csv`,
`calibration_reliability_train_vs_validation.csv`,
`rules_vs_ml_failure_analysis.csv`. The script re-verifies
`verify_training_freeze()` before reading anything, so this diagnosis cannot
run against a drifted or tampered evidence chain.

Population sizes (device-level, `device_authorization_counts.csv`): training
has 8,000 devices (4,800 normal_standard, 400 normal_bad_luck, 1,200
flash_standard, 400 flash_hard_retry, 480 attack_burst, 360 attack_evasive,
360 attack_patient); the development validation population has 2,000 devices
in the same proportions (1,200 / 100 / 300 / 100 / 120 / 90 / 90). Both were
generated by the same frozen Phase 1 generator/config, so the scenario mix is
proportionally identical -- confirmed device-for-device in
`device_authorization_counts.csv`.

## 1. Why do `normal_bad_luck` and `flash_hard_retry` devices trigger review/block?

`feature_distribution_comparison.csv` shows both groups have a low
`prospective_requests_60s` (median 1) -- they are not fast -- but
`flash_hard_retry` has `same_card_retry_ratio_24h` median **1.0** (every retry
reuses the same card, as expected for a real shopper hitting a temporarily
declining card during a flash sale) and `normal_bad_luck` accumulates enough
low-level decline/retry signal over a day to cross the model's threshold at
its tail. `rules_vs_ml_failure_analysis.csv` shows the *rules* engine flags
very few of them (`rules_only_flagged` + `both_flagged` = 13/400 training,
6/100 validation for `normal_bad_luck`; 3/400 training, 1/100 validation for
`flash_hard_retry`) -- rules are not the driver. The ML model is: at
review-threshold 0.45, `fp_fn_by_scenario_at_review_threshold_0_45.csv` shows
57/400 (train) and 31/100 (validation) `normal_bad_luck` devices, and 29/400
(train) / 11/100 (validation) `flash_hard_retry` devices cross that threshold.
These are legitimate customers whose *aggregate* retry/decline pattern over a
day looks, to a model trained on request-count and decline features alone,
statistically similar to a slow attacker. This is the same failure the
closeout documents structurally (`policy_007` fails `normal_bad_luck` review
by 1/100; `policy_023` fails `flash_hard_retry` review by 1 and
`normal_bad_luck` review by 26); this diagnosis explains the *mechanism*.

## 2. Why do rules miss `attack_evasive` and `attack_patient`?

`feature_distribution_comparison.csv`: both subtypes have
`prospective_requests_60s` median **1** and `distinct_cards_24h` median
**1-2** -- by design they never trip the rules engine's velocity/diversity
thresholds (`RAPID_REQUEST_VELOCITY` needs ≥4/60s, `CARD_DIVERSITY` needs ≥3
distinct cards/24h). `rules_vs_ml_failure_analysis.csv` confirms this
directly: `rules_only_flagged` is **0** for both subtypes in both
populations, and `both_flagged` is a small minority (18/360 train, 4/90
validation for evasive; 93/360 train, 25/90 validation for patient) -- almost
all detection of these subtypes is `ml_only_flagged`. The rules engine has no
per-request signal that separates "one low-velocity attempt from an evasive
attacker" from "one low-velocity attempt from a legitimate shopper," by
construction; it can only catch these subtypes if a device's *later* attempts
eventually trip a threshold, which patient/evasive attackers are specifically
designed to avoid.

## 3. Is the frozen HGB candidate's *ranking* quality adequate, independent of the infeasible policy?

`candidate_oof_metrics.csv` (already-frozen): candidate
`hist_gradient_boosting__04` has training-OOF PR-AUC 0.867 and
worst-subtype-coverage 0.994 at its own diagnostic threshold -- the best of
all 6 trained candidates. `grouped_oof_metrics_by_fold_and_scenario.csv`
extends this per-fold, per-subtype: `attack_evasive` per-authorization-event
recall at a flat 0.5 threshold is stable across folds (0.714-0.732, all 5
folds), but `attack_patient` recall varies markedly by fold (0.543 to 0.744,
fold 2 lowest). This is a real ranking-quality gap the aggregate PR-AUC
hides: on a per-event basis (not per-device-max), patient attackers are
frequently scored below 0.5, and one training fold generalizes to them
noticeably worse than the other four. Ranking quality is good but not
uniform across subtypes/folds, and per-event scores understate what a
device-level policy sees because policy decisions use the running maximum
across a device's attempt history, not a single event's score.

## 4. Would isotonic calibration improvement over training be leakage-free and real?

`calibration_reliability_train_vs_validation.csv` compares training-OOF
reliability bins against the same bins computed on the development
validation population using the *same frozen, already-calibrated* model (no
refitting). The two reliability curves track closely: the top bin
(0.9-1.0 predicted) has observed attacker rate 0.975 on training-OOF and
0.971 on development-validation-development, and the 0.8-0.9 bin is 0.192
(train) vs 0.024 (validation) -- the one bin with a real gap, but on a small
population (52 train devices, 41 validation devices in that bin) where a
handful of device counts swing the rate substantially. Isotonic calibration
was already frozen at training time using independently evaluated device
groups (per `phase2_final_closeout.md`); this diagnosis finds no evidence of
leakage in the existing calibration, and no strong evidence that recalibrating
would change validation-population reliability materially given how few
devices fall in the disagreeing bin.

## 5. What features dominate the `normal_bad_luck` / `flash_hard_retry` false positives?

Cross-referencing `feature_distribution_comparison.csv` with the rules
engine's own feature set: `flash_hard_retry`'s defining feature is
`same_card_retry_ratio_24h` = 1.0 (by construction -- these are shoppers
retrying the *same* card during a flash sale after declines), which is not
in `MODEL_FEATURE_COLUMNS` at all as a rule condition beyond
`CARD_SWITCH_AFTER_DECLINE` (which specifically requires *switching* cards,
so a same-card retrier should not trip it) -- meaning the ML model's false
positives on this group come from decline-adjacent count features
(`prior_decline_streak`, `prior_attempts_5m/1h/24h`) that are elevated for
any repeatedly-declined shopper regardless of intent. `normal_bad_luck`'s
false positives are driven by the same decline/attempt-count family
accumulating over a day without any single suspicious event -- there is no
feature in the current set that distinguishes "unlucky but honest" from "an
attacker with occasional real attempts," which is the structural reason both
groups sit in the same score range as slow attackers.

## 6. Is there correlation instability that would make a Phase 2B model unstable?

The already-frozen `artifacts/v2/training/eda/training_high_correlation_pairs.csv`
and `training_fold_feature_stability.csv` cover this at the feature level
(unchanged by this diagnosis). This diagnosis adds fold-level *outcome*
stability: `grouped_oof_metrics_by_fold_and_scenario.csv` shows
`attack_patient` recall-at-0.5 varying by 20 points across folds (0.543 to
0.744) while `attack_evasive` and the legitimate-group false-positive rates
are stable (within 2-3 points across folds). The instability is concentrated
in exactly the subtype that is hardest to separate from legitimate behavior,
consistent with a genuine small-sample/near-boundary effect rather than a
data leakage or pipeline bug.

## 7. Can legitimate retries be distinguished from card-switching attackers with current features?

Partially. `card_switches_after_decline_24h` and `distinct_cards_24h` already
separate `attack_burst` cleanly (median `distinct_cards_24h` = 3, versus 1 for
every legitimate and slow-attack group) -- `rules_vs_ml_failure_analysis.csv`
shows `attack_burst` is caught by rules-or-ML jointly in 460/480 (train) and
114/120 (validation) devices with `neither_flagged` = 0. But
`flash_hard_retry` (same-card retry) versus `attack_patient` (single-card,
low-velocity) are not well separated by the currently engineered
features -- both have `distinct_cards_24h` median 1 and low
`prospective_requests_60s`; the only structural difference the generator
encodes is timing/session pattern across a longer window, which is exactly
category 8 below.

## 8. Would cross-session, longer-window, approval/checkout, amount-continuity, or IP-rotation features help?

The current feature set already includes `sessions_7d`, `prior_attempts_7d`,
`cross_session_cards_24h/7d`, and `ip_changes_24h`/`devices_per_ip_5m` (per
`data/v2/development/events_with_features.csv`'s 48 columns), but nothing
longer than 7 days, and nothing that ties a device's approval/checkout
*outcome* history into features (only `prior_successful_checkouts` and
`seconds_since_successful_checkout` exist, and neither feeds the rules
engine). Given that `attack_patient` is explicitly designed to spread activity
over time and still evades a meaningful fraction of per-event scoring
(question 3), a longer window (14-30 day) and an amount-continuity feature
(does this device's spend pattern resemble its own history, versus a
probing sequence of small amounts) are plausible, evidence-motivated
additions -- not yet validated, since validating them requires new
development data or fresh validation data, which this phase does not
generate.

## 9. Do review and block need different evidence?

Yes, based on the existing closeout numbers alone: `policy_023` (closest ML
candidate) reviews all attacker devices with zero legitimate blocks beyond a
handful, but is infeasible purely on the *review* budget for
`normal_bad_luck`/`flash_hard_retry` -- i.e., the threshold needed to *review*
100% of evasive/patient attackers is already too aggressive for the
legitimate-retry population, before block is even considered. This is
consistent with treating review as a lower-cost, higher-recall action (broad
net, human or downstream disposition) and reserving block for a
higher-confidence combination of evidence (e.g., ML score plus rule
corroboration), rather than using one threshold for both actions -- which is
exactly the separate-threshold structure the frozen policy grid already
encodes (`review_threshold`/`block_threshold` as distinct parameters), just
not yet at a feasible operating point.
